# Problem Statement

In today's fast-paced world, personal safety remains a paramount concern, particularly for women, marginalized groups, and vulnerable individuals. Despite advancements in mobile technology, traditional methods of seeking help during an emergency are fundamentally flawed:

## 1. The Time Constraint
During a sudden attack, harassment, or medical emergency, victims rarely have the luxury of time to unlock their smartphones, open a dialer app, remember an emergency number, and place a call. These crucial seconds can mean the difference between safety and tragedy.

## 2. Inability to Interact Physically
In many high-stress or dangerous situations, an individual might be physically restrained, incapacitated, or too terrified to actively manipulate their phone. A safety system that relies purely on manual input (like pressing a button) is insufficient in these critical scenarios.

## 3. Lack of Immediate Context
When a call for help is made, responders and loved ones often lack immediate context. "Where are they?" and "What is happening?" are questions that take time to answer. Without real-time location tracking and audio/visual evidence, rendering effective assistance is incredibly difficult.

## 4. The Bystander Effect
Relying solely on strangers in public spaces can be unreliable. Victims need a direct, unbreakable line of communication to their own trusted inner circle—people who will immediately recognize the severity of the situation and act without hesitation.

**The core problem is the gap between the onset of an emergency and the successful dispatch of contextual, actionable alerts to those who can help.** SafeHer-AI was conceived to bridge this exact gap.
